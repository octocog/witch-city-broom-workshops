The broom making workshop

Ninety minutes at the bench, one full size broomcorn broom, and the thing you made riding home with you.

What you make

A real broom, not a craft kit. A hardwood handle, a head of natural broomcorn wound and bound with waxed cord, shoulders stitched flat so it keeps its shape, and a sweep trimmed clean. It stands about four feet tall and it works.

Then you make it yours. Choose the broomcorn color, the ribbon, a charm for the binding, and a tag with your name and the date. Most people hang theirs by the door. Some of them sweep with it.

Everything is included in the $135: the handle, the corn, the cord, the trim, the charms, the ribbons, and a maker at your elbow the whole time.

How the ninety minutes go

No experience needed. Every tool is on the bench when you arrive. Our makers work the room so nobody falls behind.

  1. 0:00

    Pick your handle

    Natural, stained or painted. Choose the broomcorn color that goes with it.

  2. 0:15

    Wind and bind

    Lay the corn around the handle and pull it tight with waxed cord. This is the part people remember.

  3. 0:40

    Stitch the shoulders

    Flatten the broom and sew it through so it holds its shape for years.

  4. 1:05

    Charms and ribbons

    Trim the sweep, then dress the binding with ribbon, a charm and a tag that says who made it.

  5. 1:30

    Fly home

    Your broom is finished and ready to carry. We can box it for the plane.

Who it suits

Anyone who wants to leave Salem holding something they made. Couples, friends on a weekend, parents with kids ten and up, people who have never held a needle and people who sew every day.

It is indoors, it is seated for most of the ninety minutes, and it does not need strong hands. The winding takes a firm pull, and a maker will help with that part if you want it.

Twelve makers at most per session. If you are more than six, a private session gives you the room to yourselves.

  • Ages 10+kids make alongside an adult

What to bring

Nothing. Wear something you do not mind getting a little dusty, since broomcorn sheds. Tie back long hair for the winding. Leave the big bags at the hotel, the benches are close together.

Getting it home

A finished broom is about four feet long and light. Most people carry it down Essex Street like a trophy. If you are flying, ask us to box it. It fits a checked bag or ships from the Broom Co. shop.
