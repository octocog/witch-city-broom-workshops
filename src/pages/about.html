About the workshop

A bench in downtown Salem where visitors make the broom they came to buy.

Why we teach it

Witch City Broom Co. has been making broomcorn brooms by hand on Essex Street for years. The question we heard most at the counter was not the price. It was how. People wanted to see the corn go on and feel the cord pull tight.

So we opened a second door at 32 Derby Square and put the bench on the customer's side. Ninety minutes later they walk out with a broom nobody else made. That is the whole idea.

The workshop is part of the Witch City Experiences family, alongside Witch City Walking Tours and the Broom Co. shop. Many visitors do all three in a day.

The craft

Broomcorn is a sorghum grown for its long seed heads. Wound tight around a handle and bound with cord, it makes a broom that outlasts anything on a supermarket shelf. The flat stitched broom you see in old New England kitchens is the same one you make here, by the same steps.

We use natural and dyed broomcorn, hardwood handles turned locally, and waxed cord. Nothing on the bench is glued. Every broom is held together by tension and thread, which is why it lasts.

Salem's tie to the broom is older than the tourist season. Brooms stood by every colonial hearth here long before the trials made the town famous. Yours will stand by a door somewhere far from here and say Salem every time someone walks past it.
