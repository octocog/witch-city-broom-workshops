A rainy day in Salem: what to do indoors

Ten things worth the walk when the weather turns.

Published November 13, 2026, by the workshop

Salem is a walking town, and a wet day can feel like it takes half the trip away. It does not. Most of what makes the place worth visiting is behind a door, and the doors are close together. Here is how we would spend one, in walking order from the train station.

1. The Peabody Essex Museum

Start here. The PEM is one of the oldest museums in the country and far bigger inside than the Essex Street entrance suggests. Give it two hours. The maritime galleries and the Yin Yu Tang house are the ones people talk about afterward.

2. Coffee on Essex Street

The pedestrian mall has half a dozen places to dry off. Pick the one with a window seat and watch the umbrellas go by.

3. The Witch City Broom Co. shop

At 246 Essex Street. Finished brooms, whisks and take home kits, and a counter where you can ask how they are made. Which brings us to the next stop.

4. Make a broom

Two blocks away at 32 Derby Square, our workshop runs most days and is entirely indoors. Ninety minutes at a bench, warm and dry, and you leave carrying something you made. On a rainy Saturday it is the best seat in town, so book before you come.

5. Old Town Hall

Right beside the workshop on Derby Square. One of the oldest surviving town halls in the country, with exhibitions and performances most days in season.

6. The Witch House

The only building still standing in Salem with a direct link to the trials of 1692. The tour is indoors and short, and the house itself is the point.

7. The Salem Witch Museum

The one at the top of the common, with the line out front. It is a sit down presentation, so it is a good place to rest your feet.

8. Lunch at the Market

Derby Street and the waterfront have the sit down places. Pickering Wharf is a short walk and has views of the harbor even in the rain.

9. The Custom House

Part of the national historic site on Derby Street. Hawthorne worked here and wrote about it in the introduction to The Scarlet Letter. Free, indoors, and quieter than anything on Essex Street.

10. A walking tour, rain or shine

If the rain eases, Witch City Walking Tours runs in all weather and the stories are better with the streets emptied out. Bring the umbrella. Bring the broom too, it has a handle.

Planning a visit? Parking, trains and the ferry are on the visit page. Sessions in October fill weeks ahead.
